\section{Paired statistics}
\label{ssec:statistics}

The main paper states the paired effects and their direction; the exact
tests live here. All three tests are exact two-sided binomial tests on the
discordant pairs (exact McNemar), not the chi-square approximation --- the
discordant counts are far too small for it. Under the null, the instances
that changed verdict between the two arms are equally likely to have changed
in either direction. Every input is a committed, already-scored outcome; the
tests are computed by the packaged statistics tool from the evidence pack,
with no model run.

\paragraph{The label-image substitution \rid{G01, G02}.} Both label arms
were scored on the same matched instances, so the comparison is paired by
construction. At top-1, replacing the isolated point-splat renders with real
capture RGB crops moves matched-instance accuracy from 1/21 to 12/21
\rid{C01, C02}: 12 instances fixed, 1 regressed, 13 discordant pairs, exact
two-sided $p = 0.0034$ \rid{G01}. At top-3 the move is 4/21 to 18/21
\rid{C03, C04}: 14 fixed, none regressed, $p = 0.00012$ \rid{G02}. The scope
is \texttt{oracle\_free\_component\_eval}: the denominator of 21 is
oracle-selected, and nothing here is end-to-end performance.

\paragraph{The identity substitution \rid{G06}.} Over the ten scored
relation questions, substituting human-supplied identity for the delivered
labels moves the same stored edges from 0/10 \rid{F40} to 7/10 \rid{F35}:
7 items fixed, none regressed, exact two-sided $p = 0.0156$ \rid{G06}. Both
the direction and the exclusivity matter --- no item was answered by the
delivered graph that human identity missed. Because one side consumes human
identity, the scope is \texttt{identity\_oracle}: the test says the arms
differ; it does not say anything deployable achieves 7/10. Its ten items
come from two rooms with one non-blind reviewer, and the $p$-value inherits
every limitation of that key.

\paragraph{The clustering limitation.} The 21 label instances sit within
only three scenes, and the instance-level tests assume an independence that
does not hold across a scene: instances in one room share a capture, a
lighting condition and a reconstruction. These are instance-level
statements, not scene-level or dataset-level ones.

\paragraph{The scene-level statement.} All three scenes move in the same
direction under the label-image substitution. Treating the scene as the
cluster, a sign test over the three scene clusters gives $p = 0.25$, so
\textbf{no scene-level significance is claimable} --- three clusters cannot
reach significance under any outcome. The scene-level evidence is
directional consistency, nothing stronger, and the paper claims nothing
stronger.
