\begin{abstract}
We report a scoped decomposition of spatial question answering over real handheld room
captures. Rather than a single accuracy number, we measure each pipeline stage against a
scope discipline separating what a system \emph{could} express from what a deployable
path can \emph{reach}, and find the two far apart.

Replacing only the labeler's input images --- real capture crops instead of point-splat
renders, with weights, vocabulary, threshold, evaluator, delivered partitions and
matching all fixed --- raises matched-instance top-1 from 1/21 to 12/21 \rid{C01, C02}
and top-3 from 4/21 to 18/21 \rid{C03, C04} across three scenes with no tuning between
them. Paired per instance the change is one-directional \rid{G01, G02}. This is a
component evaluation on an oracle-selected denominator, not end-to-end performance.

On a relation benchmark over two rooms, the \emph{same stored relations} answer 7 of 10
scored items when identity is supplied by a human \rid{F35}, 0 of 10 from learned labels
\rid{F40}, and 2 of 10 through an oracle-free grounding bridge \rid{F45} --- a paired
substitution sizing a bound, not a system improvement \rid{G06}. Relation serialization adds no measurable loss relative to recomputation under the same AABB convention: a replay reading only serialized edges agrees with recomputed geometry on
12 of 12 items \rid{F63}. The information is present in the
store and was not reached by any of the three identity paths we instantiated; the binding
stage is identity.

On a previously untouched room, direct multiview RGB answers 5 of 10 with \textbf{zero
wrong answers} \rid{F76, F77}, declines all three non-co-visible items \rid{F79}, and
fails both predeclared gates \rid{F82, F83}.

The contribution is the decomposition and its scope discipline, with negative results
committed as first-class artifacts --- not a solved room-understanding system.
\end{abstract}
