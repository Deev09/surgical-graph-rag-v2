\begin{abstract}
The object of study is evaluation methodology for question answering over 3D scene
graphs built from handheld RGB-D captures: when a system answers a spatial question
wrongly --- or not at all --- which pipeline stage lost it, and what is any reported
number entitled to claim? On our development slice the diagnosis is concentrated. Loss
collects at identity grounding: the same stored relations answer 7 of 10 scored
questions under human-supplied identity and 0 of 10 under the pipeline's own labels,
while relation availability and serialization consistency are measurably clean on the
tested items (semantic correctness of the stored relations was not independently
established). The contribution is \textbf{StageReach3D}, a scope-aware evaluator that
declares each arm's route through a stage DAG, derives reachability from declared gating
dependencies, reports reached, pass, fail and unknown separately, and enforces scope in
code: a deployable trace refuses to consume an oracle-fed stage. Its attribution is
validated by evaluator-masked artifact-level fault localization ---
\SRFaultLocalized{} injected faults across \SRFaultClasses{} fault classes and
\SRFaultRelationTypes{} relation types localized to the correct stage while the
evaluator was masked to the injected class --- and demonstrated on ARKitScenes (per-arm
reachability) and on Replica multi-relation QA (schema and outcome transfer, where
outcome-keyed reporting keeps true-empty items from masking the error rate on answered
items). The held-but-unreachable finding is scoped: under one named model
(claude-opus-5), one frozen pass, the strongest deployable path answered 5 of 10 on a
blinded one-scene pilot with zero wrong answers and failed both predeclared transfer
gates, so no cross-room claim is made. The evaluator, its schema and its protocol
transfer; every number is instance-bound.
\end{abstract}
