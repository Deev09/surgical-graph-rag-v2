\begin{abstract}
End-to-end accuracy for 3D scene-graph question answering obscures where
answerable questions are lost and whether component measurements support
deployable claims. We introduce StageReach3D, a scope-aware evaluator that
declares each evaluated arm's route through a fixed stage DAG, derives
reachability from gating dependencies, reports reached, pass, fail, and
unknown separately, and prevents deployable traces from consuming oracle-fed
stages. Evaluator-masked fault injection localizes \SRFaultLocalized{}
controlled faults spanning \SRFaultClasses{} fault classes and
\SRFaultRelationTypes{} relation types to the mutated stage. On an
ARKitScenes handheld RGB-D development slice, the dominant measured loss is
identity grounding: the same stored relations answer 7 of 10 scored
questions with human-supplied identity and 0 of 10 with the pipeline's
labels. Stored-edge replay agrees with recomputation on all 12 authored
items under the shared NEAR convention, establishing serialization
consistency but not independent semantic correctness. An oracle-free
grounding bridge recovers 2 of 10 answers and fails all predeclared gates.
Using one fixed, single-pass direct-RGB comparator (Claude Opus 5),
performance is 7 of 10 on the development rooms but 5 of 10, with zero
wrong answers and five abstentions, on a blinded one-scene pilot. Both
transfer gates fail, so we make no cross-room claim. On Replica
multi-relation QA, the evaluator's schema and outcome-aware reporting
transfer across four scorecards while preventing true-empty items from
masking errors on answer-expected items. StageReach3D turns a pooled score
into inspectable, scope-bounded failure attribution; all empirical findings
remain instance-bound.
\end{abstract}
