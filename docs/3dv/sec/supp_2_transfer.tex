\section{Transfer protocol: the frozen text and the full amendment history}
\label{ssec:transfer}

The main paper discloses that the transfer-test question generator was
amended twice before run~2 was scored. This section inlines the frozen
protocol and the complete amendment record. For anonymity, commit hashes are
replaced here by neutral event labels (``the freeze point'', ``the
amendment-1 record'', ``the key-recording point'', ``the response-pinning
point'') and repository paths by generic artifact names; the de-anonymised
transfer-protocol record, with hashes and paths restored, accompanies the
camera-ready.

\subsection{The protocol text at the freeze point}

The following restates the protocol text committed at the freeze point, at
which time the test scene had never been inspected. The restatement keeps
the protocol's own historical wording --- including ``untouched'' and
``transfer'' --- which predates the main paper's pilot framing; both gates
later failed, so no transfer claim rests on any of it.

\paragraph{The question.} Does the fixed direct multiview RGB QA path
transfer to one untouched real handheld room, with no prompt, view-selection
or scoring changes? This is a transfer test of an existing path. It is not a
graph, grounding or segmentation experiment, and none of those stages runs.

\paragraph{The scene, and its predeclared fallback.} The primary is a named
ARKitScenes capture, not present locally at freeze time; only the assets
needed for RGB packet generation are downloaded (the low-resolution wide RGB
frames, the trajectory, and the per-frame intrinsics). A fallback capture is
fixed in the same document so it cannot become scene-shopping later: if the
primary cannot be obtained, a second named validation capture is used ---
already present in a local dataset copy and never touched by any experiment
in the project: no packet, no key, no entity manifest, no graph, no mention
in any report. The substitution, if it happens, is recorded in the result
with the reason. No third option exists: if neither scene is usable the test
does not run, and no scene is chosen after seeing data. Either way the scene
stays uninspected until the protocol is committed.

\paragraph{Frame selection --- reused verbatim, not re-tuned.} The survey
rule of the existing review-kit generator: 18 equal temporal bins across the
capture, and inside each bin the frame with the highest answer-free
information score (contrast plus edge density, penalising clipping). The
score cannot know what any question is about. This is the same rule the
previous two packets used, unchanged.

\paragraph{Question generation --- fixed first, applied after the packet
exists.} The failure mode this guards against is authoring questions the
model can answer. The procedure removes the author's discretion at the point
where that bias would enter.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Anchor enumeration, three independent blind passes.} Three
  agents each receive only the 18 selected frames and list human-nameable
  objects. They do not see each other's lists, any question, any prior
  result, or anything from the project. An object becomes an \emph{anchor}
  only if at least two of the three passes list it. Single-pass sightings
  are discarded, including the author's.
  \item \textbf{Anchor ordering, mechanical.} Anchors are ordered by the
  frame index of their first appearance ascending, ties broken
  alphabetically. This ordering, not judgement about answerability,
  determines which anchors are used.
  \item \textbf{Fixed template allocation.} Questions are allocated by
  template and filled from the ordered anchor list: three presence or
  cardinality items from the anchors ranked 1--3; three comparative-distance
  items from ordered triples over the anchors ranked 1--6; two cross-view
  relation items from the two lowest-combined-rank anchor pairs sharing no
  frame. Eight questions. If fewer than two non-co-visible pairs exist
  naturally, the shortfall is recorded and the set drops to 7 or 6 --- no
  substitute pair is invented, and the ``if naturally present'' condition is
  reported either way. If fewer than six anchors survive step 1, the test
  does not run.
\end{enumerate}

Excluded by construction: internal object identifiers, annotation-derived
wording, graph-internal state, anything RGB cannot observe
(direction/orientation, support, containment, metric magnitude), and any
counting question without a stated convention.

\paragraph{Conventions, stated before the scene is seen.}
\emph{Counting}: an instance is a physically separate object of the named
class; two objects of the same class in different parts of the room count as
two even if they never appear in one frame. This is stated because a
previous key got it wrong: an earlier room was keyed as having one trash
can, and it has two physically separate waste containers at opposite ends of
the room, which all four arms then ``failed''. \emph{NEAR}: two objects are
near when the nearest points of their surfaces are within about one metre;
comparative questions are ordinal and use no threshold. Both conventions
appear verbatim in the model prompt and on the author-reviewer sheet, so the two are
answering the same question.

\paragraph{Prompt and answer schema --- composed from existing text, not
rewritten.} The prompt is assembled from the two prompts already in use,
with no rewording: presence/cardinality forms take the representation kill
test's integer/boolean phrasing; comparative and binary-near forms take the
relation challenge's phrasing, including the corrected outcome-flag wording.
The response schema records, per item, an id, an outcome (\texttt{answer} or
\texttt{unknown}), the answer, a confidence in $[0,1]$, and evidence frame
ids drawn only from the 18 supplied ids. The response must pin the packet
hash.

\paragraph{Human review.} One self-contained offline sheet, same generator
family as the relation-challenge review kit. For each question the author-reviewer
records the answer; ambiguous yes/no; evidence visibility as 0, 1, or 2+
views, judged independently of the answer; and free-text notes. The author-reviewer
also confirms or corrects the counting convention for any class the
questions count. There is no UID mapping panel --- there are no delivered
entities for this scene and none will be produced.

\paragraph{Scoring.} Exactly once, after the blinded response is
hash-pinned. Exact accuracy is correct over scored items, where scored
excludes items the author-reviewer marked ambiguous; answer coverage is (correct +
wrong) over scored items; ambiguous items are excluded from both and
reported.

\paragraph{Predeclared gates --- both required.} Exact accuracy $\ge 0.60$
and answer coverage $\ge 0.80$. Plus, as conditions on the run rather than
numbers: no prompt, view-selection, question, convention or scoring
adjustment after the scene is inspected; and one run only.

\paragraph{Stopping rule.} If either gate fails, or any condition above is
violated: the demo remains a fixed evaluation replay and no transfer claim
is made. Do not retune and re-run, do not try a third scene, and do not
soften a gate.

\paragraph{Interpretation limits.} A pass supports exactly one sentence:
under a fixed procedure, direct multiview RGB transferred to one previously
untouched ARKitScenes handheld room. It does not support generalization, a
claim about handheld capture in general, a claim about room understanding,
or any comparison to the graph path --- which is not run here and whose
existing results stand unchanged. One room, 6--10 questions, one blinded
response, one human reviewer.

\paragraph{Not run.} Instance segmentation, mask proposal, grounding, graph
extraction, the live API. No existing result, key, packet, report or demo
artifact is modified.

\subsection{Amendment 1 --- run voided and regenerated (2026-08-23)}

Run~1 is void. Nothing from it is scored, and no transfer claim rests on it.

\paragraph{Why, and why this is not tuning.} The blinded model never ran in
run~1. No response was requested, no answer existed, and nothing was scored.
The no-adjustment rule exists to prevent selecting on outcomes; there was no
outcome to select on. What was fixed is a question-generation defect flagged
before the author-reviewer answered. The run-1 key is preserved inside the
amendment-1 record: its tracked location was later overwritten by the run-2
key (10 items, none ambiguous), so the run-1 key survives only in the
version history at the amendment-1 record, where it remains the evidence
that the defect was real.

\paragraph{What the defect was.} The author-reviewer marked 4 of 8 items ambiguous,
leaving $n = 4$: one cardinality, two presence, one comparative, and zero
cross-view. Two failures follow. First, the coverage gate became an
anti-abstention gate: at $n = 4$, coverage $\ge 0.80$ requires 4 of 4
answered, so a single honest \texttt{unknown} would fail the run --- the
gate was written to catch a model abstaining its way out of being wrong,
and at this sample size it instead punishes the exact behaviour the product
path is built on. Second, the surviving mix could not support the claim:
half the scored set was presence --- the easiest and least spatial category
--- with no cross-view item at all. Root cause of the exclusions: six of
eight questions said ``in this room'', and the capture is a
$9.99 \times 13.53$\,m single-storey multi-space floor with a staircase and
adjoining rooms, not one room. The phrase does not denote, so several items
had no determinate answer.

\paragraph{The three fixes, fixed before regenerating.}
(1)~\emph{Scope wording}: cardinality and presence questions now say ``in
the captured space'', defined in the conventions as everything visible
anywhere in the supplied views; comparative and near questions need no scope
and are unchanged. (2)~\emph{Anchor ordering}: was first-appearance
ascending, which put a small wall print --- 2 of 3 passes, seen in 3 frames
--- at rank~0, where it carried three of eight questions; now ordered by
passes agreeing descending, then frames-seen descending, then first
appearance ascending. Still entirely mechanical, still nothing to do with
whether any system can answer. (3)~\emph{Ten questions, not eight}, for
headroom against exclusions: 3 presence/cardinality, 4 comparative, 3
cross-view; cross-view gains a slot because it lost both last time and it is
the category the work turns on. Everything else is unchanged: the 18-view
selection, the prompt composition, the answer schema, the conventions
themselves, the gates, the stopping rule, one run only, and the
interpretation limits.

\paragraph{The anchor passes are reused, not re-run.} The three blind
enumeration passes are reused verbatim. They enumerated objects from the 18
frames with no access to any question, any wording, or any ordering rule, so
none of the three fixes touches what they saw or reported.

\paragraph{Gate arithmetic at the new size.} At $n = 10$, accuracy
$\ge 0.60$ needs 6 correct and coverage $\ge 0.80$ needs 8 answered, so up
to two honest abstentions are affordable. If exclusions again drive the
scored set below six items, the run is void and reported as such rather
than scored --- recorded in advance so it is a rule and not a judgement
made afterwards.

\subsection{Amendment 2 --- relational slots restricted to unique-referent
anchors}

Applied immediately after amendment~1 regenerated, before any blinded
response existed, and before the author-reviewer was asked to review anything.

Amendment~1's ordering fix promoted the best-attested anchors --- but
best-attested turns out to correlate with \emph{numerous}. Of the top twelve
anchors, exactly one had a unique referent. Six of the ten regenerated
questions read ``the cushion'', ``the framed picture'', ``the wall power
socket'', where the three passes counted four, about nine, and three. A
singular definite description with no unique referent is ill-posed in the
same way ``in this room'' was ill-posed on a multi-room floor. It is
detectable without any model output, and it would have collapsed the scored
set a second time.

The rule: comparative and cross-view slots draw only from anchors where all
three passes agreed there is exactly one. 27 of the 48 admitted anchors
qualify, so the constraint costs nothing. Presence and cardinality slots are
untouched --- ``how many framed pictures are in the captured space'' is a
good question precisely because there are several. Nothing about
answerability enters: the filter is a count agreed by three observers who
never saw a question.

\paragraph{A limit on amending.} This is the second structural amendment.
Repeated amendment is itself a risk --- at some point ``fix the generator''
becomes ``iterate until the questions look right'', which is the behaviour
the protocol exists to prevent. So: no further structural amendment to this
generator on this scene. If run~2's returned key again drives the scored set
below six, that is reported as a finding about the fixed-procedure generator
--- that it does not produce a usable question set on this capture --- and
the transfer test is abandoned rather than amended a third time.

\subsection{What protects the result, and blinding stated precisely}

\paragraph{What protects the result.} The blinded model never ran in run~1
--- no response was ever requested --- so nothing scored was selected on an
outcome, and both amendments addressed defects visible in the questions
alone.

\paragraph{Blinding, stated precisely.} For the transfer run the key was
recorded at the key-recording point and the response hash-pinned at the
response-pinning point eight minutes later, so commit order does \emph{not}
prove the response predates the key. What the record establishes is that
the response was produced in a context that never received the key, and was
pinned by hash before any score was computed. The protection is isolation
plus the hash pin, not version-history ordering.

Run~2 was scored exactly once (2026-08-23); the scored outcome and both gate
failures appear in the main paper, and the per-item rows are in the packed
transfer score report in the evidence pack.
