\section{Independent annotation}
\label{ssec:annotation}

The evaluation keys used by the main results were authored by a single
reviewer who is one of the authors and is not blind to the hypothesis, and
the paper says so wherever it matters. To measure how reproducible those
keys are, an independent human annotator (blinded to all system
outputs, to the arm names, and to the hypothesis) answered the 12 authored
relation questions from a self-contained review sheet presenting the same
frozen packet frames the blinded model received, in seeded-shuffled order:
the 10 items eligible for the primary evaluation, and the 2 excluded items,
retained deliberately so that the \emph{exclusion} judgement itself is
measured for reproducibility rather than assumed.

Of the \SRAnnScoredOf{} scored items, \SRAnnScoredAgree{} agree exactly.
\SRAnnAbstainDisagreements{} of the 4 disagreements are annotator
\emph{abstentions} (``cannot determine'', confidence 1 of 5); the fourth is
a set over-inclusion at confidence 3 (the returned near-set adds the anchor
object itself alongside the key's single member). The annotator marked
neither owner-excluded item ambiguous: one was answered confidently and one
received ``cannot determine''. Ambiguity-flag agreement is
\SRAnnAmbiguityAgree{}/\SRAnnAmbiguityOf{}; the two differences are
exactly the owner-excluded items. This is evidence of partial, not
complete, key reproducibility.

To quantify how much the headline comparison depends on whose key scores it,
we re-score the arms' frozen answers -- unchanged, from the committed
per-arm traces -- under three keys (\cref{tab:key-sensitivity}): the original
adjudicated key ($n{=}10$), the second annotator's key exactly as returned,
and the agreement-only subset of items on which the two annotators returned
the same answer ($n{=}6$). Under the returned key the annotator's four
``cannot determine'' items are abstentions carrying no key answer; the frozen
scorer excludes them from that key's denominator ($n{=}8$) through the same
branch that already excludes the key author's two ambiguous items under the
original key. The rule is applied uniformly to every arm, and no new scoring
branch was written for this analysis. The ordering that motivates the paper
survives all three keys: the delivered-label graph scores 0 under every key,
the grounded graph 1--2, the two human-identity arms (the geometry control
and the stored-edge replay) retain most of their correct answers (5--7), and
the 7-versus-0 separation between stored edges under human identity and the
delivered graph persists as 5-versus-0 under both alternative keys. The keys are not interchangeable in every
respect, and we flag the one movement: under the returned key and the
agreement subset, direct RGB moves strictly ahead of the two human-identity
arms (7/8 and 6/6 against 5/8 and 5/6), where the adjudicated key has them
tied at 7/10. The gap is coverage, not new errors: under those keys the
human-identity arms record no wrong answers but abstain on set and
comparative items that direct RGB answers, including one item the original
key excludes as ambiguous but the second annotator answered -- direct RGB
answered it too, and the stored-graph arms did not. Direct RGB's single error
under the returned key is a set item on which its returned set differs from
the annotator's. No arm, prompt, or key was retuned in response to the second
annotation.

Referent descriptions and free-text relation labels are preserved verbatim
in the committed returned form for post-hoc analysis. The adjudication
overlay is itself a committed artifact and never overwrites the returned
form; every equivalence ruling carries a note. The returned review is
stored as evaluation-key reproducibility evidence and calibration data; no
key was altered in response to it for any result in this paper, and the
evaluated system was not retuned on it.
