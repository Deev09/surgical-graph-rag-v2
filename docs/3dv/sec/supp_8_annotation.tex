\section{Independent annotation}
\label{ssec:annotation}

The evaluation keys used by the main results were authored by a single
reviewer who is one of the authors and is not blind to the hypothesis, and
the paper says so wherever it matters. To measure how reproducible those
keys are, an independent human annotator --- blinded to all system
outputs, to the arm names, and to the hypothesis --- answered the 12 authored
relation questions from a self-contained review sheet presenting the same
frozen packet frames the blinded model received, in seeded-shuffled order:
the 10 items eligible for the primary evaluation, and the 2 excluded items,
retained deliberately so that the \emph{exclusion} judgement itself is
measured for reproducibility rather than assumed.

Of the \SRAnnScoredOf{} scored items, \SRAnnScoredAgree{} agree exactly.
\SRAnnAbstainDisagreements{} of the 4 disagreements are annotator
\emph{abstentions} (``cannot determine'', confidence 1 of 5); the fourth is
a set over-inclusion at confidence 3 (the returned near-set adds the anchor
object itself alongside the key's single member). The annotator marked
neither owner-excluded item ambiguous: one was answered confidently and one
received ``cannot determine''. Ambiguity-flag agreement is
\SRAnnAmbiguityAgree{}/\SRAnnAmbiguityOf{}; the two differences are
exactly the owner-excluded items. This is evidence of partial --- not
complete --- key reproducibility.

Referent descriptions and free-text relation labels are preserved verbatim
in the committed returned form for post-hoc analysis. The adjudication
overlay is itself a committed artifact and never overwrites the returned
form; every equivalence ruling carries a note. The returned review is
stored as evaluation-key reproducibility evidence and calibration data; no
key was altered in response to it for any result in this paper, and the
evaluated system was not retuned on it.
