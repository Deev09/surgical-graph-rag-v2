\section{Conclusion}

A representation can hold information sufficient to answer questions that the deployable
query paths we instantiated do not reach. We measured that gap directly: the same stored relations
answer 7 of 10 with human-supplied identity and 0 of 10 with the identity the system
actually produces \rid{F35, F40}, while the serialized edges reproduce recomputed
geometry on all 12 tested items \rid{F63}. Identity grounding is the dominant measured
loss on this \texttt{NEAR} slice; relation serialization adds no measurable loss on those
items. We claim nothing broader. Nothing here clears relation
\emph{extraction}: only that serialization added no measurable loss relative to
recomputation under the same convention, on twelve items, over human-resolved delivered
objects.

What the decomposition establishes, in order:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt
  \item \textbf{Real RGB substantially improves the label stage on oracle-matched
  instances} \rid{C01, C02, C03, C04} --- a component evaluation on an oracle-selected
  denominator, \textbf{not end-to-end or deployable performance} --- with a
  development-scene control that supports, but does not prove, the object-texture
  reading \rid{C17, C18}.
  \item \textbf{Stored 3D relations contain useful answers for human-resolved objects,
  and current deployable grounding reaches few of them} --- 7/10 \rid{F35} against 0/10
  \rid{F40} and 2/10 \rid{F45}, with serialization consistent on this slice \rid{F63}.
  \item \textbf{Direct RGB is the strongest current answer path for visible evidence} ---
  7/10 at 0.90 coverage \rid{F50, F53}, though wrong on 2 of 10 on those rooms \rid{F51}.
  \item \textbf{Neither deployed path yet handles genuinely non-co-visible questions
  reliably} --- the graph arm cannot reach them \rid{F40}, and RGB declines them on the
  pilot room \rid{F79}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The contribution is an evaluation-method one, and narrower than
``decomposition''.}
Stage-wise attribution in 3D-VL is prior work~\cite{huang2025beacon3d}, as is cross-view
necessity~\cite{mo2025mvscanqa}. What we add is a conjunction that appears unoccupied:
the relation store is \textbf{checked for serialization consistency} against geometry
recomputed under the same AABB convention \rid{F63, G05} before anything is claimed about
it --- a bounded check, since a shared convention catches a plumbing error but cannot
detect a wrong convention --- and \textbf{identity is the only varied variable}, the same
serialized edges queried under three identity sources \rid{F35, F40, F45, G06}, where
prior oracle contrasts swap the whole input modality and model. A third element,
\textbf{scoring a decline as an outcome distinct from a wrong answer}, is a protocol
proposal rather than a validated contribution: ``5 correct, 0 wrong, 3 declined''
\rid{F76, F77, F79} is not expressible in the benchmarks above, but proposals are
validated by adoption, and ten authored items cannot demonstrate that.

We do \textbf{not} claim that representations in general hold unreachable information,
that \emph{no} deployable path could reach it, or any ordering among 7, 2 and 0. The
defensible statement is bounded to this pipeline, these two rooms, and the three identity
paths we instantiated. We report where spatial information is gained (naming, from real
image evidence), retained (serialized relations, with no measured loss on the tested
items), and lost (identity grounding, and instance delivery upstream of it). Every
negative result stands as measured; none was retuned or re-run.
